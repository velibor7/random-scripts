A. There are very few occurrences of mutations relative to normal samples.
Anomaly detection is particularly useful in scenarios where anomalies (mutations, in this case) are rare compared to normal instances. This aligns with the nature of anomaly detection, which focuses on identifying rare events that deviate significantly from the majority (normal) data.

C. You expect future mutations to have different features from the mutated samples in the database.

Unsupervised anomaly detection methods do not rely on prior knowledge of anomalies. They work on the assumption that anomalies will be different from normal instances in a significant way. If future mutations have different features from known mutations, it supports using an unsupervised method as it can detect novel anomalies not seen during training
